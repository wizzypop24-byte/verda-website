Reading a supplement label in 60 seconds

28 October 2026 · 4 min read

The front of a supplement bottle is advertising. The back is the part that has to be accurate. Here is the order to read it in.

1. Serving size

Everything on the label is measured per serving, not per capsule. If the serving is two capsules and you take one, you are getting half of every number.

Check how many servings the bottle contains. It is the honest way to compare price.

2. Elemental amount vs compound

For minerals, the label may show the compound weight rather than the elemental amount. “Magnesium citrate 500 mg” is not 500 mg of magnesium.

Look for the elemental value, or the percentage that the compound contributes.

3. The ingredient list order

Ingredients are listed by weight. If a “blend” hides individual amounts, you cannot tell whether the studied ingredient is present at a meaningful dose.

Prefer labels that print every ingredient and every amount separately.

4. Other ingredients

This line covers fillers, binders, anti-caking agents and sweeteners. A short list is not automatically better, but unnecessary additives are a sign of cost-cutting.

Watch for added sugar in gummies and large amounts of sugar alcohols in powders.

5. Claims and warnings

A food supplement may describe structure or function — “supports normal muscle function” — but it may not claim to treat or cure a disease.

Read the warning line for age limits, pregnancy advice, allergen information and the “not for medicinal use” statement.

Bottom line

Serving size, elemental dose, ingredient order, other ingredients, warnings. Sixty seconds, in that order, and most of the marketing disappears.

If a label makes that hard, that is information too.

This article is general information, not medical advice. Supplements are foods, not medicines.
